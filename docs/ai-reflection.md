# AI Reflection

How AI was used in producing this work, and how I think about using it in the role.

## What AI was used for

I used AI as a thinking partner throughout this challenge. Specifically:

- **Industry benchmark lookups.** For example, mobile loading abandonment thresholds and SaaS trial conversion patterns. AI was useful for surfacing references quickly.
- **Pressure-testing the prioritisation framework.** I asked AI whether the three-dimension scoring model (impact, frequency, effort) was robust against alternative frameworks. It surfaced a few alternatives (RICE, MoSCoW, weighted shortest job first) which I considered and rejected for this scope.
- **Editorial polish.** AI helped tighten prose, find redundancy, and suggest clearer phrasings.
- **Structuring this repository.** I asked AI to challenge the file structure I was proposing and to flag what was missing.

## What AI was not used for

- **Observations.** Every friction point in this analysis is something I encountered directly while using the product on web (Chrome, macOS) and mobile (iOS) in April 2025.
- **Prioritisation decisions.** The scoring on each issue is mine. AI cannot score impact and frequency for a product it has not used.
- **Persona definition.** The first-time UK individual user persona was my deliberate choice, made before any AI involvement.
- **The 30-day plan.** The week-by-week sequencing reflects how I would actually approach the role.

## Where I overrode AI

Several times. Two examples worth flagging:

- AI suggested framing one recommendation around "user delight". I removed it. Delight is a vague, marketing word and a poor fit for an Issue tracker.
- AI suggested adding a fourth solution tier ("Visionary") on each recommendation. I removed it. Three tiers (Quick win, Medium-term, Strategic) is enough discipline for a 2-hour test.

## How I would use AI in this role

I think of AI as a tool that speeds up analysis, not as a replacement for judgement. In the Customer Experience Generalist role I would expect to use it for:

- Drafting Concierge responses that I then edit for tone and accuracy.
- Triaging recurring patterns across tickets, with my own review of the clusters before drawing conclusions.
- Surfacing context (relevant Issues, prior discussions, similar past cases) faster than manual search.
- Pressure-testing my own writing and recommendations before posting.

I would not use it for:

- Deciding what is true about a customer's experience. Direct evidence (logs, tickets, conversations) beats inference.
- Producing public-facing answers without my review.
- Substituting for a real conversation with engineering or product when the topic warrants one.

The framing I keep coming back to: AI is a tool that makes the candidate or the colleague faster. It is not the candidate or the colleague.
